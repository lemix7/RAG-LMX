The Problem

General-purpose AI
has two critical gaps.

01

No access to your files

Tools like ChatGPT are trained on public data up to a cutoff date. They have no visibility into your private reports, manuals, or internal documents.

02

Hallucination

When the model lacks information, it fills the gap with confident-sounding guesses. In academic or professional settings, this is a serious reliability problem.

→

The fix

Supply the AI with the right passages from your own documents at query time. It reads what you give it — the model becomes a reader, not a guesser.

Core Concept

Retrieval-Augmented
Generation — RAG.

Instead of asking the model to recall facts from its training, RAG fetches the most relevant passages from your document store at the moment you ask. Those passages become the model's reading material — it synthesises an answer from them rather than generating one from memory.

  • ✓Every answer is tied to a real passage in your files
  • ✓Each response shows document name and page number
  • ✓Works on private, current files you control
  • ✓If the answer isn't in your documents, the model says so

How It Works

Three steps:
load, ask, then answer.

1

Loading your documents

You upload a file. The system reads it, cuts it into small overlapping sections, and converts each section into a vector embedding — turning text into numbers that capture its meaning. Those vectors are stored in a local database called ChromaDB.

PDF · DOCX · TXT · CSV · MD 800ch chunks · 150ch overlap ChromaDB · local
2

Entering your question

You type your question. It goes through the same embedding process — converted into a vector and compared against all the stored document vectors to find the sections closest in meaning. Simultaneously, a keyword search scans for exact term matches.

query → vector semantic similarity search + BM25 keyword scan
3

Generating the answer

The two result sets are merged with weighted scoring, then a local neural reranker scores every candidate and keeps only the most relevant. Those top passages are handed to GPT-4o-mini, which writes the final answer.

60% semantic · 40% keyword CrossEncoder reranker top 5 → GPT-4o-mini
⚡

Going beyond basic RAG: most systems stop at a single search. We run two searches in parallel, merge them with weighted scoring, then apply a neural reranker to filter out anything that isn't genuinely relevant before the AI sees it.

Why Hybrid?

One search method
always leaves something out.

Semantic search alone

  • Better for general meaning — "deadline" retrieves "due date", "cutoff"
  • Handles paraphrasing and synonym variation well
  • Struggles with rare or exact strings: codes, IDs, names
  • Can rank a loosely related passage above a precise one
+

BM25 keyword search alone

  • Essential for specific terms — unique names, product IDs, article codes
  • Deterministic and fast — no embeddings required
  • Blind to synonyms and rephrased questions
  • Scores purely on word frequency, not meaning

Stage 3 — Reranking

The CrossEncoder
cuts noise before the AI reads.

  1. 1

    After the ensemble merge we hold up to 20 candidate passages.

  2. 2

    The CrossEncoder reads each (question, passage) pair jointly — unlike vector search, which compares two independent embeddings. It produces a fine-grained relevance score for each pair.

  3. 3

    Passages scoring below the threshold are dropped entirely, regardless of their rank in stage 1.

  4. 4

    The 5 highest-scoring passages are forwarded to GPT-4o-mini as context.

Verifiable, source-cited answers

Every response includes the document name and page it came from. No hallucination. The reranker runs locally at zero extra cost.

Chunk size, ensemble weights, top-k, model names — all configurable in config.py without touching pipeline logic.

Throughout this project, my main focus was making the retrieval process as accurate as possible to minimise hallucination. Every design decision — the hybrid search, the ensemble weights, the CrossEncoder reranker — was driven by one goal: ensuring the model only reads passages that are genuinely relevant before it generates a single word.
